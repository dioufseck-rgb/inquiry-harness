Without that bridge, composing would either broaden the narrower C13 population or treat a recommendation as measured evidence. Answer: The question is not settled as posed. The main issue (Q1) has no accepted claim: the net-benefit claim C2 and the balance claim C3 are both out or undecided. The accepted evidence is a component finding. The USPSTF (2022) concluded with moderate certainty that initiating low-dose aspirin in adults 60+ without CVD and not at increased bleeding risk has no net benefit, and it recommends against initiation (C13, C14). The comparator in that judgement is no aspirin. The retrieved trial evidence leans the same way, but it is indirect and second-hand. ASPREE (70+, 65+ for some, placebo) reported about 1 percentage point more major hemorrhage, 3.8% vs 2.8%, HR 1.38 (1.18-1.62), in the study evidence (E20). Net benefit is not shown, and net harm is not established. The USPSTF verdict is 'no net benefit', not 'net harm'.
