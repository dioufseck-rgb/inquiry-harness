# Does initiating low-dose aspirin for primary prevention reduce net cardiovascular harm in adults aged 60 and over?

Starting low-dose aspirin for primary prevention in adults 60 and over is not shown to lower net cardiovascular harm. The evidence leans toward harm or zero net benefit. C1 is accepted: excess major bleeding equals or exceeds the MI and ischemic stroke events prevented. The main trial evidence is ASPREE (adults 70+, about 4.7 years), where major hemorrhage rose from 2.8% to 3.8% (about 10 extra per 1,000) and there was no disability-free survival gain. The weighting that supports this is equal or bleeding-weighted. Under equal weighting, C18 holds that the net is harm or zero. A severity-weighted small benefit (C2, C19) has no supporting argument. The size of the net balance is not quantified for the 60+ band. The only per-1,000 netting is all-age pooled data (NNT 265 vs NNH 210, about 1 per 1,000 net bleeding excess, no CI). ASCEND, a diabetes population, gave the opposite sign under equal weights (about 11 prevented vs 8.9 bleeds per 1,000). Both pooled figures include ASPREE, so they are not independent. No 60+ subgroup with net benefit was found. USPSTF 2022 recommends against initiation at 60+ (Grade D) on a moderate-certainty conclusion of no net benefit. This guideline position rests on one origin, a lifetime QALY model that uses the same pooled inputs.

## Key judgements

- **moderate** Initiating low-dose aspirin in adults 60+ increases net cardiovascular harm, or at best gives no net benefit, with bleeding at least equal to prevented events; the claim leans on ASPREE (70+) and the pooled all-age data, and is not directly measured at 60-69. (C1)  
  rests on: E25/E56 (ASPREE major hemorrhage, about 10 per 1,000) and E24 (no disability-free survival gain); the 70+ trial is carried to the 60+ band, and the 60-69 model result ranges from slightly negative to slightly positive (E39).
- **moderate** Under equal weighting of MI, stroke and major bleeding, the net effect over about 5 years is harm or zero, not benefit; the margin is about 1 per 1,000 with no interval in all-age pools. (C18)  
  rests on: E50 pooled NNT 265 vs NNH 210 (secondhand, not age-restricted, no CI) plus the ASPREE mortality excess (E2, E59), which is borderline (CI 1.01-1.29).
- **moderate** In ASPREE, aspirin did not significantly reduce CV events in adults 70+, though the exact hazard ratio is not documented in the evidence read. (C7)  
  rests on: E54, a secondhand summary stating no difference in CVD events; the HR of about 0.95 is not in the retrieved evidence.
- **moderate** ASPREE showed higher all-cause mortality driven by cancer deaths (HR 1.14, 1.01-1.29) in older adults. This is a borderline, post hoc secondary finding that has not been replicated. (C12)  
  rests on: E2 and E3, a single trial; pooled younger-population trials found no mortality effect (E35, E19).
- **moderate** USPSTF 2022 recommends against initiating aspirin at 60+ (Grade D) with moderate certainty of no net benefit. ACC/AHA 2019 advises against routine use over 70 or at increased bleeding risk, but permits consideration at 40-70 in select high-risk patients. (C21)  
  rests on: E28, E46 and E42 (the ACC/AHA position relayed secondhand); ESC and the original ACC/AHA text were not retrieved in this inquiry.
- **low** No 60+ subgroup with net benefit was shown. Benefit rises with baseline risk, but bleeding rises with age and offsets it, and the evidence for a benefit subgroup concerns ages 40-59. (C3, C15, C16)  
  rests on: E39 and E51 against C3/C16; C3 is not accepted, and C15 has no accepted support, so this is a lean and not a settled finding.
- **low** The size of the bleeding and CV-event effects in the 60+ band is not settled. C9's 2-5 per 1,000 range is understated versus ASPREE (about 10 per 1,000), and the CV benefit estimate (C6) has no supporting argument. (C6, C9, C10)  
  rests on: A31 rebuttal of C9; E34 gives relative effects only, with no absolute events per 1,000.

## What would change this

- An age-restricted (60-69 and 70+) pooled analysis with absolute MI, stroke and major bleeding per 1,000 and confidence intervals showing prevented events exceeding bleeds under equal weights.
- A 60-69 specific balance, or a microsimulation sensitivity run with a delayed colorectal cancer benefit, that turns the 'slightly negative to slightly positive' result clearly positive.
- Long-term ASPREE follow-up showing a delayed cancer incidence or mortality reduction, or a replication of the cancer-death excess in another older cohort.
- A 60+ high-risk, low-bleeding-risk subgroup analysis showing net benefit.
- The primary ASPREE CV composite estimate (HR and CI) and intracranial hemorrhage counts, which would ground C7's figure and C10.

## Unresolved

- Per-1,000 net balance specifically for ages 60-69 is not established; the evidence is mostly ASPREE (70+) and all-age pools.
- Whether the sign depends on the weighting (C5) is not settled: no argument supports a severity-weighted benefit, but none was tested quantitatively.
- Absolute CV benefit per 1,000 (C6) and the intracranial hemorrhage excess (C10) are not quantified in the documents read.
- ESC 2021 guidance and the original ACC/AHA 2019 text were not retrieved in this inquiry; C21 rests on USPSTF and a secondhand ACC/AHA summary.
- The cancer-mortality excess is post hoc and exploratory, and the effect of statin use and diabetes on the balance is not resolved (ASCEND's diabetes data is not restricted to 60+).
- The evidence is not independent: the pooled analysis includes ASPREE, ARRIVE and ASCEND, and the microsimulation uses the same pooled inputs.
