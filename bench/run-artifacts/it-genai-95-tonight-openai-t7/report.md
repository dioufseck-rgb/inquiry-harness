# Is it established that about 95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots deliver no measurable return?

No—the approximately 95% prevalence claim is not established for enterprise generative-AI pilots generally by the evidence gathered. Its source is MIT Project NANDA’s 2025 report, The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025, which says that “95% of organizations are getting zero return” and separately that 5% of integrated AI pilots are extracting millions in value; those are different units of analysis [C21, C22]. The evidence supports treating this as a report-specific headline rather than a validated general pilot failure rate: the retrieved passages do not identify its pilot-level denominator or establish how the stated six-month ROI method applies to the headline cases [C6, C20]. This does not establish a lower alternative rate, nor does it establish that the reported outcomes were measured zero or negative net returns.

## Key judgements

- **moderate** The 95% figure is genuinely asserted in the originating Project NANDA report, but establishing that assertion does not validate its empirical prevalence [C21]. (C21)  
  rests on: E1’s executive-summary wording, with the primary extract’s authenticity defaulted in A2; the secondary accounts repeat the same originating report.
- **moderate** The report’s 95% statement concerns organizations, whereas its adjacent 5% statement concerns integrated AI pilots, so the two statements alone do not establish complementary pilot outcome rates [C22]. (C22)  
  rests on: E1 directly records both statements and their different units; this is one documentary origin, not independent prevalence corroboration.
- **moderate** The stated research inputs—more than 300 publicly disclosed initiatives, interviews with representatives of 52 organizations, and surveys of 153 senior leaders—do not establish a representative pilot-population estimate [C6, C20]. (C6, C20)  
  rests on: E2 and E15 describe the research inputs, E18 records selection and comparability limitations, and A9 supports the unresolved linkage between those inputs and the headline denominator.
- **moderate** Although a six-month post-pilot ROI method is stated, its application to every case underlying the 95% headline is not established by the retrieved passages [C20]. (C20)  
  rests on: A9’s limited documentary finding, particularly E65 and E69 on research inputs; E17 supplies the six-month method but does not resolve its connection to the headline cases.
- **low** Whether the headline category combines unmeasured or undetected returns with measured zero or negative returns remains unsettled, as do its net-cost accounting and exclusive use of financial outcomes [C10, C11, C12]. (C10, C11, C12)  
  rests on: The withdrawn A4 inference cannot establish classification rules: E20’s observation-based interview questions and E8’s measurement concern are compatible with other classification procedures, as A8 explains.

## What would change this

- An underlying outcome table or author clarification identifying the 95% calculation’s numerator, denominator, eligibility criteria, and unit of analysis would resolve the principal denominator uncertainty.
- Evidence linking every headline case to the six-month post-pilot assessment, with a defined ROI formula, implementation and operating costs, and department-size adjustment, would resolve the measurement-period and accounting questions.
- Coding rules and case classifications separating unmeasured, unknown, undetected, measured-zero, negative, and positive returns would determine what the headline actually counts.
- A documented sampling and generalization procedure, together with uncertainty specifically attached to the 95% estimate, could support—or undermine—its extension to enterprise pilots generally.
- An authenticated correction or revised report changing the quoted units, headline wording, or research-input descriptions would change the accepted documentary findings [C6, C20, C21, C22].

## Unresolved

- The population prevalence remains unsettled: neither approximately 95% nor the proposed alternative of fewer than 90% is established by this inquiry [C1, C2].
- The exact pilot-level numerator and denominator, overlap among the research inputs, and derivation of 95% remain unresolved [C7, C20].
- The operational definition of return, success threshold, treatment of missing outcomes, net-cost accounting, and uniform application of follow-up remain unresolved [C10, C11, C12, C14].
- An uncertainty interval specifically for the 95% estimate and its resampling specification were not retrieved in this inquiry; the report’s general bootstrap statement does not settle that question [C16].
- Independent, directly comparable pilot-return estimates were not established in this inquiry; derivative coverage of NANDA should not be counted as independent replication [C17].
- Critic requests for underlying calculations, coding protocols, case-level follow-up, sampling documentation, and reconciliation of Fortune’s differing interview and survey counts remain ungrounded by the retrieved materials.
