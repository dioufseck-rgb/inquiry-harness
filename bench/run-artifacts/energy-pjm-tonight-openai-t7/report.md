# Can PJM serve projected data-centre load growth in Northern Virginia through 2030 without new dispatchable generation?

The evidence gathered does not settle whether PJM can reliably serve Northern Virginia’s projected data-centre growth in every year through 2030 without new dispatchable generation: neither feasibility nor unavoidable failure is accepted (C1, C2). The assessed scenario uses PJM’s January 2025 forecast and the fleet operating on 31 December 2024, prohibits subsequent fuel-burning additions, uprates and behind-the-meter generators, permits non-fuel-burning additions and storage, and excludes imports attributable to new external fuel-burning generation. The evidence points toward reliability concerns rather than an assured yes, but the retrieved results do not establish this counterfactual’s annual adequacy or Northern Virginia’s local deliverability (C16–C22). A defensible capacity, energy or transmission shortfall for that scenario therefore remains unquantified.

## Key judgements

- **low** Firm service through 2030 remains undecided because neither the feasibility claim nor its failure alternative has supporting argument establishing the specified forecast, restricted portfolio and annual reliability outcomes (C1, C2). (C1, C2)  
  rests on: Neither claim has an accepted load-bearing node; the decisive missing observation is a matched annual resource-adequacy and local transmission-security assessment.
- **low** The retrieved PJM-wide adequacy results cannot determine the no-new-fuel-burning verdict because Synapse’s scenarios include 9 GW of new gas during 2025–2030, while PJM’s reported study-portfolio margins are not disaggregated into the stipulated restricted portfolio (C20, C21; E17, E66). (C20, C21)  
  rests on: E17 establishes the scenario mismatch, and E66 does not isolate the required counterfactual; neither adequacy claim is accepted.
- **low** Northern Virginia’s delivery feasibility remains separate and undecided because the retrieved material does not establish annual contingency-secure margins with scheduled reinforcements or with those reinforcements delayed (C16–C19). (C16, C17, C18, C19)  
  rests on: The decisive evidence would be Northern Virginia network cases identifying thermal, voltage and stability results, reinforcement need dates and secure load limits.
- **low** Interruptible service and alternative definitions of dispatchability remain unproven routes to feasibility because modeled flexibility and resource classifications do not establish that enforceable local interruption, storage or excluded external supply changes the verdict (C3, C5, C13). (C3, C5, C13)  
  rests on: Matched reliability sensitivities and enforceable customer and import commitments are needed to resolve these claims.
- **low** Conditional deployment rules and competing Three Mile Island restart dates do not establish that a resource actually credited by 2030 lacks a necessary development arrangement, while the revised modeling-disclosure finding itself remains undecided in the evaluated graph (C25). (C25)  
  rests on: E11–E12 and E177–E183 disclose assumptions, but the surviving A3 and A4 undercuts require linking selected resources to dated outstanding prerequisites.

## What would change this

- A matched assessment showing compliance in every year from 2025 through 2030 with both PJM resource-adequacy requirements and Northern Virginia transmission-security requirements under the stated prohibition would support a conditional yes (C1, C20).
- A matched assessment showing a reliability breach in at least one year after crediting the specified retention, non-fuel-burning resources, storage, transmission upgrades and qualifying imports would support a conditional no (C2, C21, C22).
- Verified January 2025 forecast tables and utility submissions providing annual coincident peaks, energy, hourly profiles, connected-demand screening and Northern Virginia delivery allocations would resolve the load baseline and growth alternatives (C6–C10).
- A unit-level baseline-to-2030 ledger documenting retirements, retention commitments, commissioning dates, resource-specific accreditation, storage duration and firm-import provenance would resolve the available-resource alternatives (C11–C14).
- Contingency studies showing secure delivery limits with, without and following delays to named Northern Virginia reinforcements would determine the delivery conditions and required connection restrictions (C16–C19).
- Matched sensitivities varying load growth, retirements, project dates, enforceable interruption and the inclusion of storage, external generation and backup generators in the prohibition would establish how the verdict changes (C3–C5, C13, C20–C24).
- Selected-resource records matched to dated outstanding development prerequisites would ground the project-readiness concern; competing schedule reports or build eligibility alone would not do so (C25).

## Unresolved

- Q1–Q5 have no accepted answer: overall feasibility, geographically resolved demand, available resources, local transmission capability and annual system adequacy remain undecided.
- Annual capacity margins, expected unserved energy and secure Northern Virginia delivery margins for the stipulated counterfactual were not established in this inquiry.
- The amount and operating conditions of enforceable Northern Virginia data-centre flexibility, including recovery demand and reliance on backup generation, remain unresolved (C3).
- Whether dispatchability definitions or allowing new fuel-burning generation changes feasibility remains unresolved (C4, C5).
- The project-selection-to-outstanding-prerequisite links requested by the critics were not retrieved, including the relevant Three Mile Island milestone records (C25).
- Independent provenance of overlapping deployment assumptions across the Synapse and Pennsylvania reports was not established; different report titles should not be treated as independent project-readiness verification (C25).
