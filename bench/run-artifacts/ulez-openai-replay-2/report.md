# Did the August 2023 expansion of London's Ultra Low Emission Zone reduce roadside NO2 concentrations in outer London?

The causal answer is not settled by the evidence evaluated here: neither a reduction, an increase nor zero change is established for the 2024 annual mean at newly covered outer-London roadside sites (C1–C4, C13–C15). The retrieved official analysis points toward a modest reduction: it estimates 1.1 µg/m³, or 4.8%, lower roadside NO2 as of September 2024 relative to no expansion (E44). But that endpoint estimate does not establish the full-calendar-2024 effect, and its numerical expansion-specific uncertainty and counterfactual validation remain unresolved. It should therefore be treated as a reported estimate suggesting a benefit, not as a settled annual causal effect; the unresolved result also does not establish that the expansion had no effect.

## Key judgements

- **low** The direction and magnitude of the full-calendar-2024 causal effect remain undecided because the retrieved September endpoint estimate does not establish the same-site annual comparison against retaining the previous boundary (C1–C4, C13–C15; E44, E77). (C1, C2, C3, C4, C13, C14, C15)  
  rests on: The period and monitoring-network mismatch between the retrieved concentration analysis and the annual causal claims; an accepted annual counterfactual comparison would change this judgement.
- **moderate** Detected outer-London vehicle compliance rose from 88.1% in November 2022 to 90.9% in June 2023 and 96.7% in September 2024, but these administrative observations do not identify expansion-induced fleet changes or an attributable NO2 reduction (C16). (C16)  
  rests on: The implementing authority's detected-compliance series, particularly E22 and E52; E148 is arithmetic derived from E22 rather than independent corroboration.
- **moderate** Separate taxi, heavy-vehicle, private-hire and bus policies operated alongside the expansion, but their independent contribution to outer-London emissions changes is not established by the policy records (C17). (C17)  
  rests on: Administrative policy dates and the reported bus milestone in E15, E93, E123, E126 and E127, all drawing on GLA/TfL administration.

## What would change this

- An expansion-specific January–December 2024 evaluation reporting observed and no-expansion annual roadside NO2 means for identified newly covered outer-London sites, aggregation weights, effects in µg/m³ and percentages, and uncertainty would allow C1–C4 and C13–C15 to be assessed.
- A validated counterfactual retaining earlier ULEZ phases and other continuing policies, with comparable meteorology, seasonality and regional background pollution, and tests of pre-expansion trends, control-area spillovers and alternative specifications, would strengthen or overturn the suggested reduction.
- Ratified, matched-site 2022 and 2024 concentrations with data capture, relocations and instrument changes would settle the descriptive annual comparison in C5–C7.
- A stable-coverage operating-fleet comparison against a credible no-expansion trajectory, spanning announcement and implementation, could distinguish expansion-induced anticipation from natural turnover and earlier-policy spillovers.
- Corrections to the underlying compliance series would change C16; corrections to the administrative policy dates or bus milestone would change C17.

## Unresolved

- The full-calendar-2024 causal direction, magnitude and uncertainty remain undecided, as does the matched-site measured change between 2022 and 2024.
- The separate contributions of meteorology, regional background pollution, traffic, ordinary fleet turnover and other policies remain unresolved.
- Numerical expansion-specific uncertainty for the reported September 2024 reduction was not retrieved in this inquiry; the all-phases uncertainty cannot substitute for it.
- The official counterfactual uses smoothed roadside-minus-background trends and a comparison area 5–40 km outside Greater London, starting in December 2022 to allow anticipation, but the adequacy of shared-trend assumptions, control contamination and adjustment for London-specific policies remains unresolved.
- The independent study's reported non-significant first-three-month result does not settle the annual effect; numerical outer-London traffic-site estimates, intervals and supplementary diagnostics remain retrieval needs.
- Critic requests for constant-camera compliance data, attributable pre-implementation scrappage replacements, geographically matched bus deployment and vehicle-kilometres, and concentration-analysis replication remain ungrounded by additional retrieved evidence.
- Independent corroboration of the accepted administrative findings remains unresolved: differently labelled GLA/TfL reports and arithmetic derived from their tables are not independent evidential origins.

### A6 — Argument from a record

**Claim.** Separate London fleet policies operated alongside the expansion: taxi licensing requirements introduced in 2018, tighter heavy-vehicle LEZ standards introduced in March 2021, a new private-hire licensing requirement effective January 2023, and an ongoing zero-emission bus programme reporting more than 1,300 buses by January 2024. These records do not establish their independent contribution to outer-London road-transport NOx reductions during 2022–2024.

**Grounds.**
- E15: Separate taxi-licensing requirements introduced in 2018 were changing the London taxi fleet alongside ULEZ: by December 2024, over 79% of licensed taxis met ULEZ standards, including over 8,800 zero-emission-capable taxis. Taxis are exempt from ULEZ and excluded from its compliance figures. — "As of December 2024, over 79 per cent of London licensed taxis are compliant with the ULEZ standards, including over 8,800 ZEC taxis"
- E93: A separate emissions policy applied to large and heavy vehicles: LEZ standards were tightened on 1 March 2021, and reported compliance increased from 96.1% in January 2022 to 97.7% in September 2024. This documents a concurrent fleet-compliance trend, not its contribution to NO2 or its independence from the expansion. — "The emissions standards for the LEZ were tightened to match the ULEZ standards on 1 March 2021."
- E123: An ongoing bus-fleet programme predating the expansion had brought the entire fleet to ULEZ standards in January 2021 and continued replacing vehicles with zero-tailpipe-emission buses, exceeding 1,300 in January 2024. — "TfL has continued to decarbonise the bus network, hitting the milestone of over 1,300 zer-emission buses in January 2024."
- E126: Taxi licensing policies established before the expansion required newly licensed taxis to be zero-emission capable from January 2018 and reduced the maximum age of the most polluting taxis between 2020 and 2022; 54% of black taxis were zero-emission capable by December 2023. — "the maximum age for the most polluting taxis was reduced from 15 to 12 years, between 2020 and 2022."
- E127: A separate private-hire vehicle licensing requirement took effect in January 2023, requiring newly licensed PHVs to be zero-emission capable and meet Euro 6 standards; this is a concurrent fleet policy that an expansion-specific counterfactual should account for. — "The latest requirement was introduced in January 2023, with all PHVs licensed for the first time needing to be ZEC and meet the Euro 6 emissions standards."

**Warrant.** A tangible record (dataset, filing, measurement, document) states the claim. (document_record)

**Backing** (critical questions):
- [answered] (assumption) Is R what it purports to be, from the stated origin and unaltered? — The evidence identifies passages and tables in the GLA/TfL London-wide ULEZ One Year Report and Air quality in London 2016–2024, with official publication URLs and page references.
- [answered] (assumption) Was the quantity measured or the fact recorded by a method adequate to the use being made of it? — These are administrative records of the authority's own licensing rules, LEZ implementation and bus programme. Such records are adequate to establish policy dates and reported fleet milestones, without using them to estimate an independent pollution effect.
- [answered] (assumption) Does R state A, or does A require an interpretation of R that could be contested? — The records identify taxi requirements introduced in 2018, tighter heavy-vehicle LEZ standards from 1 March 2021, a new-PHV licensing requirement effective January 2023, and more than 1,300 zero-emission buses by January 2024. They report policies and fleet observations, not a decomposition of their independent contributions to outer-London NOx reductions during 2022–2024.
