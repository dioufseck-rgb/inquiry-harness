# Did the August 2023 expansion of London's Ultra Low Emission Zone reduce roadside NO2 concentrations in outer London?

The evidence gathered does not settle whether the August 2023 expansion caused lower roadside NO2 across a fixed set of outer-London locations over September 2023–August 2024; all four causal alternatives remain undecided (C1–C4). The reported estimates lean toward a small reduction: City Hall’s analysis puts monthly smoothed concentrations 0.6 µg/m³ (2.3%) below its no-expansion comparator in September 2023 and 1.0 µg/m³ (4.3%) below it in August 2024 (E73). These are not a verified fixed-station annual causal effect: changing station availability, concurrent policies and counterfactual comparability limit that interpretation (E24, E62, E63). A separate study found no statistically significant effect over a three-month evaluation, which does not establish zero effect over the full year (E84; C4).

## Key judgements

- **low** A small reduction is suggested but not established for the specified annual causal contrast, resting on the reported monthly estimates rather than an accepted annual-effect claim (C1–C4; E73). (C1, C2, C3, C4)  
  rests on: E73 supplies the directional indication, but the main causal claims remain undecided because the reported smoothed zonal estimates do not establish the fixed-station annual contrast.
- **low** The reported 1.1 µg/m³ (4.8%) reduction is a September 2024 monthly-trend endpoint against City Hall’s no-expansion comparator, not the September 2023–August 2024 annual effect, whose attribution remains undecided (C1; E35, E60, E63). (C1)  
  rests on: E35 defines the endpoint estimate; E60 identifies the 5–40 km external control and possible spillovers, while E63 identifies changing monthly station availability.
- **low** The separate study’s three-month finding of no statistically significant NO2 effect cannot establish an unchanged full-year concentration, resting on its shorter evaluation and the numerical uncertainty not retrieved in this inquiry (C4; E84, E93). (C4)  
  rests on: E84 reports the shorter-period non-detection, and E93 describes an uncertainty procedure without supplying the outer-London numerical interval.
- **moderate** New treatment applies to monitored roads included inside the expanded boundary but outside the previous boundary, rather than to borough membership alone, resting on the documented expansion and a defaulted road-level interpretation (C5; E96, E78). (C5)  
  rests on: A1, supported by E96 and E78, with its road-inclusion interpretation defaulted pending verification against road-level boundary records.

## What would change this

- A fixed-station September 2023–August 2024 expansion-only estimate, in µg/m³ and percent with a numerical uncertainty interval, using a validated no-expansion comparator could establish a reduction below 5%, a reduction of at least 5%, an increase or an unchanged concentration (C1–C4).
- Validation showing comparable untreated trends and adequate treatment of weather, seasonality, concurrent policies and control-zone spillovers—while retaining expansion-induced fleet and traffic changes—would resolve key limitations of the reported causal estimates (C1–C4, C14–C17).
- Official before-and-after boundary and road-inclusion records cross-referenced to monitor coordinates could verify or overturn the defaulted road-level assignment rule and identify newly covered versus already-covered monitors (C5, C6).
- Ratified station-level observations and coverage records for both September-to-August periods could establish the fixed-panel observed change and test whether changing station composition explains the published aggregate change (C9–C13).

## Unresolved

- The sign, annual magnitude and numerical uncertainty of the expansion-only causal effect remain unsettled; this does not establish either zero effect or an increase (C1–C4).
- Named monitors’ treatment assignments, including any already-covered sites within administratively defined outer London, remain unresolved (C6).
- The specified fixed-panel before-and-after concentration changes, separately for newly covered and already-covered locations, and the contribution of monitoring-coverage changes remain unresolved (C9–C13).
- The net no-expansion trajectory and the magnitude of any anticipatory roadside NO2 reduction remain unresolved (C14–C17).
- The requested station crosswalks, ratified fixed-panel calculations, annual expansion-specific uncertainty outputs, counterfactual validation and supplementary numerical results were not retrieved in this inquiry.
- A concrete geographic mismatch between datasets was not established by the documents read; differences between cameras, traffic counters and pollution monitors do not by themselves demonstrate different geographic definitions (A3; revised C18).

### A1 — Argument from a record

**Claim.** Roadside locations on roads included in the expanded ULEZ, outside the pre-29 August 2023 boundary but inside the new boundary, became subject to ULEZ requirements on 29 August 2023; assignment depends on the monitored road's inclusion rather than borough membership alone.

**Grounds.**
- E96: The report dates the London-wide expansion to 29 August 2023, distinguishes it from the central and inner-London phases, and states that the expanded boundary coincides with the heavy-vehicle LEZ boundary; it does not identify individual monitoring roads. — "On the 29 August 2023, to bring the health benefits of cleaner air to even more people the Mayor further expanded the ULEZ to cover all London boroughs. The ULEZ boundary is now the same as the boundary for the LEZ for heavy vehicles."
- E78: The article describes the August 2023 expansion as extending ULEZ coverage to outer London, following central-London coverage in 2019 and an inner-London expansion in 2021; it does not identify individual monitored roads. — "Ulez has covered central London since 2019 and was increased to cover a wider area of inner London in 2021 before the latest expansion."

**Warrant.** A tangible record (dataset, filing, measurement, document) states the claim. (document_record)

**Backing** (critical questions):

- [answered] (assumption) Was the quantity measured or the fact recorded by a method adequate to the use being made of it? — The GLA policy overview records the implementation date and new boundary, while the BBC account distinguishes the earlier central and inner-London phases.
