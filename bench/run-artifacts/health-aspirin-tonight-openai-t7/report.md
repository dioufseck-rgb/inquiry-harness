# Does initiating low-dose aspirin for primary prevention reduce net cardiovascular harm in adults aged 60 and over?

The evidence gathered does not settle whether starting low-dose aspirin reduces, increases, or changes by less than 1 per 1,000 the five-year net risk in all adults aged 60 and older without cardiovascular disease (C1–C3 remain undecided). It leans against a net benefit, particularly in the predominantly age-70-and-older ASPREE population: over median follow-up of 4.7 years, cardiovascular-event rates were 10.7 versus 11.3 per 1,000 person-years with aspirin versus placebo, while major-hemorrhage rates were 8.6 versus 6.2. However, these separate outcomes have different definitions from the requested combined endpoint and cannot simply be subtracted to obtain a deduplicated five-year net effect (C4). Thus, a defensible absolute net magnitude for the full target population cannot be reported from this inquiry.

## Key judgements

- **low** Neither net benefit, net harm, nor equivalence within 1 event per 1,000 over five years is established for the specified population and endpoint, because the retrieved estimates do not supply the aligned patient-level first-event union needed to distinguish those alternatives (C1–C3). (C1, C2, C3)  
  rests on: E2 and E16 document endpoint and overlap limitations; E14 documents population, regimen, and follow-up mismatches, leaving C1–C3 without supporting arguments.
- **low** The direction of the retrieved ASPREE results leans against net benefit—its cardiovascular estimate was uncertain while major hemorrhage increased—but this does not establish the specified five-year net-harm threshold (C1, C2). (C1, C2)  
  rests on: E3 reports a cardiovascular-event HR of 0.95 (95% CI 0.83–1.08), and E4 reports a major-hemorrhage HR of 1.38 (1.18–1.62), in a predominantly age-70-and-older population with separate endpoints.
- **high** An equally weighted first-event composite measures whether a person experiences any included event, not total mortality, disability, or recurrent-event burden, and must distinguish stroke types and count overlapping or fatal events only once (C4). (C4)  
  rests on: The first-occurrence definition in E40, nested bleeding categories in E54 and E105, and the independently described ASCEND safety endpoint in E81 underpin the accepted classification in A1–A2.
- **low** The specified five-year cardiovascular benefits, intracranial and major extracranial bleeding excesses, and difference between ages 60–69 and 70 or older remain unestablished rather than demonstrated to be zero (C8–C15). (C8, C9, C10, C11, C12, C13, C14, C15)  
  rests on: E49, E101, and E105 provide relevant but nonmatching cardiovascular and intracranial estimates; E20 lacks aligned extracranial risks, and E157 supplies only a qualitative age gradient.

## What would change this

- A randomized comparison in adults aged 60 and older receiving 75–100 mg daily, reporting five-year cumulative risks and uncertainty for the exact deduplicated first-event composite, would distinguish C1–C3 if its risk-difference interval supported a reduction of at least 1 per 1,000, an increase of at least 1 per 1,000, or a change strictly within those bounds.
- Aligned five-year component results, including fatal bleeding separately, with results for ages 60–69 versus 70 or older and baseline cardiovascular and bleeding risks, would resolve the component and subgroup claims C8–C15.
- A specified five-year health-state analysis with explicit utilities, recurrence handling, and results alongside the unweighted composite in the same population would address C5; a demonstrated reversal under plausible alternative weights would address the weighting concern in C7.
- An implemented endpoint that sums recurrent events, assigns severity weights, or counts fatal and overlapping events more than once would require changing its interpretation from the first-event occurrence measure accepted in C4.

## Unresolved

- The direction and absolute magnitude of the five-year net effect for the full age-60-and-older population remain unresolved (C1–C3).
- Exact five-year cardiovascular component effects, bleeding component effects, fatal-event effects, and age- and risk-specific treatment differences remain unresolved (C8–C15).
- The proposed five-year QALY construction and whether plausible severity weights reverse the benefit–harm conclusion remain unresolved (C5 and C7); the retrieved lifetime modeling does not settle those questions.
- The requested original JPPP results and supplements, aligned ASPREE joint-event analysis, and ASCEND age-specific initiation analyses were not retrieved in this inquiry.
- Critic requests to clarify uncertain-type stroke and hemorrhagic transformation, inspect the commissioned decision model, and distinguish aspirin-naive participants from prior users were not fully grounded in the documents read.
- Repeated publications from ASPREE or ASCEND and the overlapping arguments A1–A2 must not be treated as independent treatment-effect corroboration.

## Surviving arguments

### A1 — Argument from definition to classification

**Claim.** An unweighted first-event composite of myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, cardiovascular death, intracranial hemorrhage, and major extracranial bleeding measures the probability of experiencing at least one included event, but does not measure the total burden of fatality, disability, or recurrent events. Its component definitions must distinguish ischemic from hemorrhagic stroke and count a fatal event only once.

**Grounds.**
- E6: The review sometimes substituted unspecified stroke counts for ischemic stroke counts, so its ischemic-stroke endpoint is not consistently restricted to ischemic events. — "If not sufficiently specified, the number of reported strokes was used."
- E13: The mortality-adjusted net clinical benefit is a weighted sum of incidence-rate differences for ischemic stroke, myocardial infarction, hemorrhagic stroke, and major extracranial bleeding. It uses ischemic stroke as the reference, rather than equal event weights, and is neither a first-person-event composite nor a QALY endpoint. — "Weights were thus w1 = 0.89 for MI, w2 = 3.23 for hemorrhagic stroke, and w3 = 0.63 for major extracranial bleeding"
- E21: The authors explicitly weighted hemorrhagic stroke three times as severely as ischemic stroke using prior mortality estimates. This illustrates a severity-weighted benefit–harm endpoint, but not a QALY health-state model or a demonstrated reversal of the treatment conclusion under alternative weights. — "Based on previous estimates [ 31 , 32 ], we weighted hemorrhagic stroke threefold worse than IS."
- E40: The reported MACE endpoint counts first events but excludes bleeding and uses coronary heart disease death rather than the broader cardiovascular-death component specified in the target composite. — "incident MACE [a composite of the first occurrence of non-fatal myocardial infarction (MI), fatal and non-fatal ischaemic stroke, and coronary heart disease death]"
- E44: Major haemorrhage includes haemorrhagic stroke, symptomatic intracranial bleeding, and clinically significant extracranial bleeding. The clinical-significance definition includes transfusion, hospitalization, prolonged hospitalization, surgery, or death. — "Clinically significant bleeding was defined as bleeding that led to transfusion, hospitalization, prolongation of hospitalization, surgery, or death."
- E45: The long-term analysis uses time to first event for each outcome separately, estimates cause-specific hazards with other deaths censored, and estimates cumulative incidence using a method that accounts for competing death. — "Cumulative incidence curves with competing risk of death were obtained using the Aalen–Johansen estimator."
- E54: The major-haemorrhage endpoint combines intracranial bleeding with extracranial bleeding meeting specified severity criteria; it is not an extracranial-only endpoint. — "Major haemorrhage is a composite of haemorrhagic stroke, intracranial bleeding, or extracranial bleeding that resulted in transfusion, prolonged hospitalization, surgery, or death."
- E81: The major-bleeding safety endpoint includes all confirmed intracranial hemorrhage, sight-threatening eye bleeding, and other bleeding requiring hospitalization or transfusion or causing death or disability. Its scope is therefore not identical to the specified intracranial-plus-major-extracranial endpoint. — "sight-threatening eye bleeding, or any other serious bleeding episode (i.e. requiring hospitalization or transfusion, or fatal or disabling)"

**Warrant.** A thing meets the definition of a category, so it belongs to the category. (definition_classification)

**Backing** (critical questions):
- [answered] (assumption) Is D the accepted definition of K in the relevant practice, or one chosen to fit? — A first-event composite classifies each patient by whether at least one included event occurs, rather than summing that patient's events or their severity. E40 explicitly defines a composite by first occurrence, and E45 describes time-to-first-event analysis. E13 and E21 document a distinct approach that explicitly assigns unequal mortality-based weights; those weights are not inherent in an unweighted first-event endpoint.
- [answered] (assumption) Does t satisfy each condition in D, on the evidence? — The endpoint proposed in C4 meets the first-event definition if it records each patient's earliest occurrence among the specified cardiovascular and bleeding events. Counting that union once per patient measures occurrence, not recurrent-event burden, disability, or severity-weighted fatality. Harmonization is necessary: E6 documents substitution of unspecified strokes for ischemic strokes; E54 shows that major hemorrhage already includes intracranial bleeding; and E44 and E81 show that serious bleeding can itself be fatal or disabling. Thus ischemic and hemorrhagic events must be distinguished, overlapping bleeding categories cannot be added as independent events, and a fatal event must not be counted again as both an event and a death. These records support the endpoint's conceptual interpretation and safeguards, not an observed estimate of the proposed combined endpoint.
- [open] (exception) Is t a borderline case where the definition does not settle membership?
