# Did the August 2023 expansion of London's Ultra Low Emission Zone reduce roadside NO2 concentrations in outer London?

The question is not settled by the evidence gathered. No claim about the size of the outer London roadside NO2 effect is accepted: C1 (1-3 µg/m3), C2 (small, interval includes zero) and C3 (exactly zero) are all undecided or out. The evidence leans towards a small effect, probably under the official 'up to 4.8%' (about 1 µg/m3 or less), but the graph cannot establish its size. One independent weather-normalised synthetic-control study (npj Clean Air 2025, hosted twice) found no significant ULEZ3 change in NO2 at traffic or background sites. That null covers only about 3-8 months, is pooled rather than outer-London-roadside, and is biased towards zero because its pre-period includes anticipatory fleet change. The operator's one-year report gives 'up to 4.8% lower' for outer London roadside, with no visible method or interval, and it has an upward-bias risk. The only claim accepted in the main evidence is that the fleet changed little beyond the pre-existing trend (C15). That claim is itself contested, because London-wide data show about 100,000 fewer non-compliant vehicles a day by September 2024.

## Key judgements

- **low** An exactly-zero effect is rejected: the independent null is too short, pooled and anticipation-contaminated to show zero, and the official estimate points the other way. (C3, C12)  
  rests on: E1 (operator's 'up to 4.8%') against E21/E41 (3-month null). Both A18 and A21/A22 stand.
- **low** The official 4.8% is only an upper bound from the scheme operator with no visible method or interval, so it cannot establish a 1-3 µg/m3 or 3-10% effect. (C1, C8)  
  rests on: E1 as the single origin; E6 is a relay of it and adds nothing. A26 and A27 stand.
- **low** A small reduction whose interval includes zero (C2) remains plausible but is unproven, because its supporting unit conversions (E56, E58) and fleet arithmetic (E78, E79) were undercut. (C2)  
  rests on: E21/E41 (non-significant result) together with E1 (upper bound), with A24, A25, A49 and A50 defeating the magnitude arguments.
- **moderate** Anticipatory fleet replacement before August 2023 (non-compliance fell from 23.1% to 7.4%) means the baseline already holds part of the effect and any post-start contrast is biased towards zero. (C4, C15)  
  rests on: E27 and E4; the anticipation test was run only for ULEZ1 (E33, E53).
- **low** The deflationary claim that the question is unanswerable is not supported: separate outer London counterfactuals were fitted, and the same design detected a 4.6% effect for ULEZ1. The difficulty is the short window and anticipation, not impossibility. (C4, C13)  
  rests on: E52 and E40; A59 and A39 stand.
- **low** The claim that concurrent factors were as large as the ULEZ effect is unsupported. Weather-normalised and counterfactual trends did not differ after ULEZ3, and nothing quantifies remote working or cost of living. (C17)  
  rests on: E42; A36 stands.

## What would change this

- The npj Clean Air Supplementary Tables S5/S6 giving an outer London traffic-site ULEZ3 estimate with a 95% interval, and the site count. A negative interval excluding zero would favour C1 or C2; an interval centred on zero with a tight width would favour C3.
- Appendix 3 of the GLA One Year Report giving the method, comparison sites, central estimate and interval behind 'up to 4.8%'.
- An independent meteorology-normalised difference-in-differences of outer London roadside sites against roadside sites in Kent, Essex, Surrey and Hertfordshire, covering at least 12 months after August 2023.
- An anticipation placebo around November 2022 or January 2023, or a re-estimate with the pre-period ending before May 2022, to test whether the null is biased towards zero.
- Outer London roadside-only mean NO2 and monthly ANPR compliance and traffic counts, to convert percentages to µg/m3 and to compare the post-start fleet change with the pre-trend rate.

## Unresolved

- Which of C1, C2 or C3 holds: none is accepted, and no observed outer London roadside estimate with an interval over 1-2 years is in the graph.
- Whether the official 4.8% carries upward design bias; its method is not visible.
- Whether outer London roadside monitoring coverage and data capture were adequate. No outer London roadside site count or capture rate was found.
- Whether traffic volumes in outer London changed (C16 has no supporting argument), and whether the fleet changed faster than trend after the start (A33, A51).
- Whether the ULEZ3 control cities (other UK cities, not the adjoining counties) track outer London before the expansion; no pre-period fit statistics were shown.
- Which sources are independent: S5 and S6 are the same study, and E1 and E6 are the same origin, so corroboration is thinner than the evidence count suggests.
- C5, C6, C11, C18 and C19 have no supporting argument and remain open.

### A14 — Argument from a record

**Claim.** The share of ULEZ-compliant vehicles observed in outer London rose after the expansion (from about 90% to about 95%), the number of non-compliant vehicles fell by roughly a third or more, and total traffic did not fall materially.

**Grounds.**
- E8: Compliance of vehicles subject to the standards was 96.7% London-wide by September 2024, and nearly 100,000 fewer non-compliant vehicles were detected on an average day than in June 2023. This supports a fleet-compliance mechanism, though not an outer London figure or a traffic volume change. — "The London-wide compliance rate ... after the first year of the expansion (as of September 2024) was 96.7%"
- E5: The report estimates that around 97% of vehicles seen in London are ULEZ compliant, and that outer London NOx emissions were 14% lower in 2024 than without the expansion (13% for cars and 16% for vans). These are modelled estimates of the emissions mechanism and are not concentration measurements. — "The latest ULEZ expansion has reduced harmful nitrogen oxides (NOX) emissions in outer London, which are estimated to be 14 per cent lower in 2024 than they would have been without the ULEZ expansion."

**Warrant.** A tangible record (dataset, filing, measurement, document) states the claim. (document_record)

**Backing** (critical questions):
- [answered] (assumption) Is R what it purports to be, from the stated origin and unaltered? — E8 reports the official TfL/GLA one-year report data, and E5 comes from that report itself.
- [answered] (assumption) Was the quantity measured or the fact recorded by a method adequate to the use being made of it? — Compliance is measured from ANPR camera detections, an adequate method for observed compliance. The figures are London-wide, not outer London only.
- [answered] (assumption) Does R state A, or does A require an interpretation of R that could be contested? — The records state a high compliance rate (96.7-97%) and about 100,000 fewer non-compliant vehicles per day than in June 2023. They do not give an outer London rate or any traffic figure. This supports the rise in compliance, not the 'about 95%' figure or the traffic part specifically.
- [open] (exception) Are the definitions and units in R the same as those assumed in A?
- [open] (exception) Has the fact changed since R was produced?

**Rebuttal.** none recorded

### A15 — Argument from a study's reported result

**Claim.** Outer London compliance and traffic volumes showed no change around the expansion beyond the pre-existing trend, so the fleet changed no faster than before.

**Grounds.**
- E26: For ULEZ3 (the August 2023 expansion), the non-compliant vehicle share had already fallen to 7.4% by implementation, and only fell a further 2.7-4.7% by September 2023. The authors say the expected NOx reduction is not detectable against the counterfactuals (their Fig. 3). This supports a null or negligible outer London effect and also anticipatory fleet change. — "Following the implementation of ULEZ3, the number of non-compliant vehicles only fell by 2.7–4.7% by September 2023 (Supplementary Table S10 ). Although this is expected to lead to reduced NO X emissions, such an impact is not detectable when considering the counterfactuals (Fig. 3 )."
- E43: Non-compliant vehicles in London were already down to 7.4% by the start of ULEZ3. After implementation the number of non-compliant vehicles fell only a further 2.7-4.7% by September 2023, which the authors say is not detectable against the counterfactuals. The authors link this to anticipatory fleet replacement, helped by scrappage and the announcement. — "Following the implementation of ULEZ3, the number of non-compliant vehicles only fell by 2.7–4.7% by September 2023 (Supplementary Table S10)."

**Warrant.** An empirical study with a stated design reports an estimate, so the quantity it estimates plausibly has that value in the setting the claim is about. (study_result)

**Backing** (critical questions):
- [answered] (assumption) Is D adequate for this kind of claim: for a causal claim, does it identify the effect by intervention, a credible control, or a defensible counterfactual rather than a before/after comparison? — The authors compare observed outcomes against counterfactuals (their Fig. 3) and read the compliance trajectory from TfL data. This is a credible counterfactual design for emissions, but it is thin for the traffic part of C15.
- [answered] (assumption) Do the study's population, setting, intervention and outcome match the claim's, or is the result being carried across to a different place, period, site type or measure? — The compliance figures are London-wide and cover all site types. They are not outer London roadside specifically, and they do not cover traffic volume.
- [answered] (assumption) Is the uncertainty of R reported (interval, standard error, sample size), and is it narrow enough to bear on the claim? — No interval is given for the 2.7-4.7% figure, and it is ambiguous between percentage points and percent. The authors only say the effect is 'not detectable'.
- [answered] (assumption) Does the source state R and D as used here, rather than a paraphrase or a press summary of them? — Both the primary paper (S5, in E26) and its published version (S6, in E43) state the authors' own summary directly.
- [open] (exception) Is there a specific risk of bias in T: selection, contamination of the comparison, anticipation, outcome measurement, selective reporting, or the authors' interest?
- [open] (exception) Do other studies of comparable design disagree with R?
- [open] (exception) Is U wide enough that values on both sides of the claim's threshold remain plausible?
